# The STR Tax Strategy ("STR Loophole")

_Snapshot: July 2026. Verify everything with a CPA before acting._

## The idea in one paragraph

Rental real estate losses are normally **passive** and can't offset W-2/active income
(IRC §469). But a rental whose **average guest stay is 7 days or less** is not a "rental
activity" under Reg. §1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)(A) — it's treated like an active business. If we
also **materially participate**, losses from the property are **non-passive** and offset
ordinary income. We manufacture a large Year-1 loss via a **cost segregation study** plus
**100% bonus depreciation**, which front-loads 25–40% of the building's depreciable basis
into Year 1. The resulting tax savings substitute for the cashflow shortfall.

## Current law (as of July 2026)

- **100% bonus depreciation is permanent** under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA,
  signed July 4, 2025) for qualifying property **acquired and placed in service after
  January 19, 2025**. No phase-down, no sunset. IRS Notice 2026-11 confirmed mechanics.
- Bonus applies to property with MACRS recovery period ≤ 20 years: the 5-, 7-, and
  15-year components a cost-seg study carves out (appliances, flooring, cabinetry
  allocable portions, landscaping, driveways, fencing), plus separately purchased
  **furniture and furnishings** (5/7-year).
- The building structure itself (27.5-year residential; 39-year if avg stay ≤ 7 days and
  substantial services put it in the hotel bucket — see below) does **not** get bonus.
- **Used property qualifies** — an existing house new to us works.
- "Placed in service" = ready and available to rent (listed), not the closing date. To
  claim in tax year N, the property must be **listed and available by Dec 31 of year N**.

## Requirements checklist (all must hold)

### 1. Average stay ≤ 7 days
Total guest-nights ÷ number of stays ≤ 7 for the tax year. Design the listing around
2–4 night minimums. **Watch out:** Solana Beach mandates a 7-night minimum and one booking
per 7-day period — that pushes average stay to ≥7 and likely fails this test. Del Mar's
3-night minimum and Encinitas' 2-night minimum are fine.

### 2. Material participation
Most practical tests for a two-earner household:

- **100 hours AND more than anyone else** (test 3) — the usual route. No single cleaner,
  co-host, or property manager can log more hours than us. This effectively rules out
  full-service property management in year 1; self-manage with hourly vendors.
- **500 hours** (test 1) — safe harbor if we can get there.
- Spouses' hours **combine** for material participation.
- Keep a contemporaneous log (dates, tasks, hours). This is the #1 audit item.

Distance matters: managing a Carlsbad property from home is far easier to substantiate
than Monterey (~6 hr away). Factor this into the market choice.

### 3. Personal use limits (§280A)
If personal use exceeds the **greater of 14 days or 10% of rented days**, the home is a
"residence," losses are disallowed under §280A, and the whole strategy collapses. With
~150 rented nights, we get ~15 personal nights. **The "second home we also enjoy" goal is
in direct tension with the tax strategy** — in loss-harvesting years, personal use must be
kept under the cap. Fix-up/maintenance days don't count as personal use if work is the
primary purpose.

### 4. Depreciable basis mechanics
- Land is never depreciable. Coastal CA land allocations are brutal — often **40–70%** of
  purchase price is land (check county assessor ratios). This directly shrinks the
  cost-seg benefit and is a major input the model exposes.
- Cost-seg studies typically reclassify **20–35%** of the *improvement* (non-land) basis
  into bonus-eligible classes. Study cost: ~$3–6k for a single-family home.
- If avg stay ≤ 7 days, the building may be **39-year nonresidential** property rather
  than 27.5-year (positions vary; ask the CPA). The model defaults to 39-year to be
  conservative on the straight-line portion.

### 5. Loss limitation stack (in order)
1. **Basis / at-risk** (§465): recourse mortgage debt generally gives at-risk basis; fine.
2. **Passive loss** (§469): avoided via the ≤7-day + material participation route.
3. **Excess business loss** (§461(l)): net business losses can offset non-business income
   only up to ~**$317k MFJ (2026, indexed)**; the excess becomes an NOL carryforward, not
   lost. Relevant if we buy at a price point that generates a mega-loss.

### 6. California does not conform
CA disallows bonus depreciation and has its own depreciation schedules. The Year-1 loss
for CA purposes is much smaller. **Tax savings in the model = federal savings (big) + CA
savings (small)**. Only the federal side sees the bonus-depreciation boost. Don't double
count.

### 7. The deduction is worth a *blended* rate, not the top marginal rate
Our profile: ~$600k household income, MFJ (~$568k taxable after the standard
deduction). A large STR loss deducts bracket-by-bracket on the way down:

| Slice of the loss | Offsets income in bracket | Federal rate |
|---|---|---|
| first ~$56k | $568k → $512k | 35% |
| next ~$109k | $512k → $404k | 32% |
| everything below $404k | — | 24% |

So a $317k loss (the §461(l) cap) saves ~$91k federal — a **28.7% blended rate**, not
37%. Two big implications:

1. **Loss dollars below ~$404k of remaining income are only worth 24 cents** federal.
   The marginal value of a bigger property (bigger loss) falls off a cliff well before
   the §461(l) cap — another point for the cheaper property.
2. **Timing matters**: Alyssa's bonus years raise taxable income, which raises the
   blended rate on the same loss. If a big-bonus year is foreseeable, placing the
   property in service (or timing the cost-seg election) in that year is worth real
   money — roughly +$3.2k of federal savings per +$10k of income above $512k
   (35% vs 32% band), and more if it pushes past $768.7k into 37% territory.

The model computes savings from actual 2026 federal MFJ brackets (Rev. Proc. 2025-32)
and CA Schedule Y brackets rather than a flat marginal rate.

## Second-order items

- **Depreciation recapture on sale**: §1245 recapture on the bonused personal property at
  ordinary rates; §1250 unrecaptured gain at 25%. This strategy is a deferral + rate
  arbitrage, not free money. Holding long-term or 1031-ing mitigates.
- **Self-employment tax**: STR income is generally NOT subject to SE tax unless we provide
  substantial services (daily cleaning, meals, concierge). Don't provide them.
- **TOT and permits**: 10–15% transient occupancy tax in these markets; price it into ADR.
- **Insurance**: coastal CA + STR use = expensive and hard (FAIR plan in fire zones,
  flood zones near lagoons). A real quote is required before any offer.
- **Financing**: a "second home" mortgage (10% down, better rate) typically **prohibits**
  rental for part of the year or requires owner occupancy; renting it on Airbnb can
  breach the occupancy covenant. An **investment/DSCR loan** (20–25% down, higher rate)
  is the clean path. Decide before modeling — it changes the down payment and rate.
- **Trust line of credit (our plan)**: borrowing against trust assets at prime + 1%
  (7.75% at July 2026) to fund the down payment/closing/furnishing. Interest is
  deductible against the STR under the **interest-tracing rules** (Reg. §1.163-8T) as
  long as proceeds are traceably used for the rental — keep the draw in a separate
  account and pay property costs directly from it. Risks: the rate floats with prime;
  interest-only carry never amortizes; and pairing it with a 75% mortgage means 100%
  financing, which adds ~$28–44k/yr of carry and makes annual break-even much harder.
  A partial draw (or paying the line down from tax-refund proceeds in Year 2) is the
  natural tuning knob.
- **Repeat years**: the bonus boost is Year 1 only. Years 2+ produce modest depreciation.
  Some investors repeat the play with a new property every year or two; otherwise expect
  after-tax performance to converge to pre-tax performance.
