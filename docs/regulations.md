# STR Regulations by Market

_Snapshot: July 2026. These rules change constantly and are parcel-specific. Confirm with
the city/county planning department (and read the actual ordinance) before writing an
offer that depends on rental income._

Legend for "strategy fit": can we legally run a non-hosted STR with average stays ≤ 7
days as a non-primary residence?

## North County San Diego

| Jurisdiction | Non-hosted STR legal? | Key constraints | Strategy fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carlsbad** | Yes, **Coastal Zone only** (plus La Costa Resort Master Plan area) | Permit + business license (Ch. 5.60); 24/7 contact w/ 45-min response; 10% TOT + 2% CTBID | **Good** — but only for parcels west of the Coastal Zone line. Verify parcel zone first. |
| **Encinitas / Leucadia / Cardiff** | Yes | Permit (~$425) + $1M liability insurance; non-hosted capped at 2.5% citywide / 4% in Coastal Zone; 200-ft separation between non-hosted STRs; 2-night minimum | **Good** — check cap availability and the 200-ft rule for the specific address. |
| **Solana Beach** | Yes, but | **7-night minimum stay**, one booking per 7-day period | **Poor for the tax play** — 7-night minimum makes the ≤7-day average-stay test marginal/failing. Fine as a pure second home. |
| **Del Mar** | Effectively no | Citywide cap of 129 permits — **full** (waitlist since May 2026); primary-residence requirement (owner lives there ≥ 6 mo/yr); 3-night min; 13% TOT | **Fails** — non-hosted investment STRs not permitted; we would not qualify. |
| **City of San Diego (Torrey Pines / La Jolla)** | Yes | Tier 3 whole-home license (lottery when oversubscribed); ≥90 rented days/yr to keep license; 10.5% TOT | **Workable** — license availability is the risk; Tier 4 (Mission Beach) closed, Tier 3 has had availability. |
| **Oceanside** | Yes (not on our list but adjacent) | Permit required; relatively permissive historically | Backup option. |

**Bottom line:** Carlsbad Coastal Zone and Encinitas are the strongest fits. Del Mar is
out. Solana Beach conflicts with the 7-day test.

## Monterey Peninsula

| Jurisdiction | Non-hosted STR legal? | Key constraints | Strategy fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carmel-by-the-Sea** | **No** (residential zones, banned since 1989) | 30-day minimum; active platform monitoring; large fines | **Fails.** |
| **Pebble Beach / Del Monte Forest (unincorp. county)** | Barely | Jan 2026 Board vote to ban STRs <30 days in unincorporated residential zones (ordinance in progress; Planning Commission rejected 8-1; litigation ongoing — *MCVRA v. County of Monterey*). Even "conditional" commercial VR permits require **Pebble Beach Company consent**, which it opposes | **Fails for practical purposes** — regulatory direction is hostile and the Pebble Beach Co. blocks permits. |
| **Carmel Valley (unincorp.)** | No | Commercial VRs banned in all residential-zoned portions | **Fails.** |
| **Carmel Highlands / Big Sur (unincorp.)** | No | Commercial VRs banned | **Fails.** |
| **City of Monterey** | **No** | <30-day rentals illegal; advertising ban | **Fails.** |
| **Pacific Grove** | Mostly no | Measure M banned most residential STRs; small license pool in Coastal Zone with waitlist | **Fails for practical purposes.** |

**Bottom line:** The Monterey Peninsula is effectively **closed** to this strategy. Only
niche paths exist (commercial-zoned parcels, licensed homestays with owner present, or
Limited Vacation Rentals ~3x/year in the county) and none support the tax play. If we
love the area, it's a second-home-only market — buy it for lifestyle, not for the STR
loss.

## Santa Barbara Area

| Jurisdiction | Non-hosted STR legal? | Key constraints | Strategy fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **City of Santa Barbara** | Narrow | Currently regulated as hotels: legal only where hotels are allowed + certain coastal zones; **prohibited in single-family zones**. New ordinance (adoption targeted Sept 2026, Coastal Commission review into 2027) creates a coastal license area + inland commercial-zone-only path; permit ~$3.3k + ~$2.9k/yr renewal | **Narrow** — only specific parcels (coastal license area / hotel zones). High regulatory churn right now. |
| **Montecito / Summerland (unincorp. SB County)** | Coastal zone: yes with permit; inland: hostile | County allows STRs in coastal zone with permit; verify current caps | **Possible** — parcel-specific; Montecito price points are extreme. |
| **Carpinteria** | Limited | STRs restricted to specific zones (beach neighborhood); permit + cap | **Possible** — small permit pool, check availability. |
| **Goleta** | Limited/hostile | Restrictive; verify current ordinance | Weak. |

**Bottom line:** Santa Barbara is a **parcel-by-parcel** market and mid-ordinance-change.
Carpinteria's beach zone and coastal unincorporated county are the realistic paths.

## Overall ranking for the strategy (July 2026)

1. **Encinitas / Cardiff / Leucadia** — legal non-hosted STRs, 2-night min, strong ADRs.
2. **Carlsbad (Coastal Zone parcels)** — clear permit path, strong demand.
3. **City of San Diego coastal (Torrey Pines–La Jolla)** — workable if a Tier 3 license is obtainable.
4. **Carpinteria beach zone / SB County coastal** — possible, thin permit supply.
5. **Solana Beach** — great town, but 7-night minimum breaks the tax test.
6. **Del Mar, all of Monterey Peninsula** — not viable for this strategy.

## Standing diligence items per candidate property

- [ ] Confirm parcel's zone + coastal-zone status on the city GIS map.
- [ ] Confirm permit availability *today* (caps, waitlists, separation rules).
- [ ] Read HOA CC&Rs — many coastal HOAs ban STRs regardless of city rules.
- [ ] Confirm the ordinance's minimum-stay doesn't break the ≤7-day average test.
- [ ] Check pending ordinance changes (city council agendas) — grandfathering matters.
